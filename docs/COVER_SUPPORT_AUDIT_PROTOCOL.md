# Protocol for auditing land cover in the 65% built comparison

The Stage 3 stable-cover rule removed the nominal high-tree endpoint at 65% built cover. Test which parts of the rule account for the loss, inspect the affected places in Sentinel-2 imagery, and evaluate support with a second land-cover model. The analysis uses the 2,442 cells with usable observations in each of 2023, 2024, and 2025 and the existing 15 Landsat dates as an availability registry. It carries forward the same built references, tree endpoints, block origin and pairing rules. [Stage 2 design](BUILT_COVER_PROTOCOL.md) · [Stage 3 results](OVERPASS_VALIDATION_RESULTS.md)

## Fixed grid and optical inputs

Select the common cells within five percentage points of 65% built cover and 2.5 points of 12.5% tree cover in at least one year. Inspect the full histories of these cells. Separately apply all combinations of the original cross-year five-point limits for Esri tree, built, and water shares to the 2,442 common cells. Count blocks and block-dates for each built band, plus the intersections that satisfy both bands on the same date.

Use Sentinel-2 tile T48NUG for June through September of each year, with the same calendar window to limit seasonal differences. Start with products having up to 65% tile cloud cover. On the study grid, retain the Level-2A scene classification codes for vegetation, nonvegetated surfaces and water with a 20 m buffer from other codes. Prefer acquisitions that give multiple observations of at least 80% of the fixed land in a cell. Give the audit cells five times the selection weight, retain at most five acquisitions per year, and require seven days between acquisitions. Match each selected scene to the original public Level-1C image by sensor, sensing timestamp, relative orbit and tile. Record the acquisition IDs and unsigned source URLs before running the classification. [Copernicus Sentinel-2 products](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/d/sentinel/sentinel-2-products-specification-document-15_1) · [Planetary Computer scene assets](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-2-l2a)

Run the publicly released Dynamic World forward model in the laboratory environment. Use the nine input bands, normalization and softmax from its example runner, with the L1C metadata offsets and 10 m bilinear alignment for coarser bands. Use the local Level-2A masks for the clear classification footprint. Average its probabilities over at least two clear acquisitions in a year. Use the most probable class at mean probability at least 0.60, and repeat the counts for 0.50 and 0.70. This is a local reconstruction using the released weights and documented sampling, with a seasonal composite and a local mask. Both Esri and Dynamic World draw on Sentinel-2 imagery. [Dynamic World data and method](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01307-4) · [Versioned model runner](https://github.com/google/dynamicworld/tree/0dd288fae5fb5291d988e5ea3c12eb91df481ec5)

## Compare support and review the imagery

Within the fixed land footprint, require both products to recognize a nonwater class on at least 80% of the native land centers for each usable cell-year. Use these shared centers for tree and built shares. Use all accepted centers, including water, for the water shares. Compare the original Esri fractions, the Esri fractions on the shared footprint, and the Dynamic World fractions on that footprint. In the primary version the pixel footprint also stays fixed across all three years. As a sensitivity check use masks that can vary by year, retaining cells that qualify in every year. Apply the five-point stability filter separately to each source. The comparison distinguishes loss from missing optical coverage, changes in the pixel footprint, and differences between labels.

The planned probability screens yielded sparse comparison support. For an exploratory diagnostic, also retain the most probable label at every pixel with at least two clear observations. This preserves the extent, two-scene requirement and original pairing rules. Tabulate how the optical, label and score conditions affect cell retention and the original Esri comparison counts.

For each of the 19 audit cells, show the fixed-stretch original reflectance, Esri labels and local Dynamic World labels for all three years. Review years on pixels clear in at least two acquisitions in each year. Flag contiguous vegetation signals where the median NDVI changes by at least 0.15 and the two observed NDVI ranges are disjoint. Record the largest connected patches in 10 m pixels. Use coherent changes in image texture and vegetation signal to interpret whether a surface change is visible. Favor an interpretable patch over isolated pixels. A modest NDVI difference and agreement between models carry limited weight for distinguishing canopy from grass, shadow, or mixed built surfaces. Mark ambiguous cases and describe the specific reason. [Dynamic World probability and cloud-mask limitations](https://datasets.wri.org/datasets/dynamic-world)

Tabulate both bands' endpoint support and their shared blocks before and after each filter. The support counts refer to the frozen geography, fixed endpoints and original local pairing rules. Retain the observed counts when support is sparse. This stage evaluates the inputs and comparability of the earlier built-cover analysis.
